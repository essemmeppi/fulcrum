# Public documents provide a starting point for Fulcrum in at least 86 countries

**Worldwide national-government screen · Research conducted 31 October 2026 · Summary prepared 1 November 2026**

Fulcrum aims to turn government documents into whole-service blueprints: the citizen-facing steps and internal work, with actors, decisions, rules, data and handoffs tied to their sources. These blueprints would provide the basis for government validation and, subsequently, executable workflows. We researched how widely the public record offers a promising starting point before a government agrees to collaborate.

**What we researched.** We ran discovery searches across all 197 countries in our registry, covering six areas: social services; revenue and business; identity and rights; land and environment; public services such as health and education; and government operations such as procurement and records administration. We then inspected selected government-authored manuals, procedures and process models, recording internal actions, responsible roles, sequence or branching, source dates and limitations. Public copies of previously disclosed manuals were included. Regional and municipal governments were excluded from the count.

**What we found.**

| Finding | Countries | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Detailed operational material | **86** | At least one named national function has inspected operational documentation with internal-process detail. |
| Promising, unresolved | **41** | Inspected material has potential, but procedural depth, adoption or applicability remains unresolved. |
| Public-facing evidence only | **20** | The inspected evidence covers the public interface; useful internal-process documentation has not been established. |
| Unknown | **50** | Leads remain unverified, access failed or the available search evidence was insufficient. |

Of the 86 countries with detailed material, **46 have evidence for a citizen-, business- or community-facing or regulatory workflow**. The other **40 currently qualify through enabling government operations**. Examples include US workplace-safety inspections, Indian business approvals, Peruvian import clearance, Australian education assistance, Fijian community relocation and New Zealand visa decisions and procurement planning.

**Depth is encouraging; breadth remains under-measured.** Detailed documents are available across very different government functions and country contexts. Most countries, however, have only one inspected example; detailed evidence across at least two service areas has been recorded in just two countries. This describes the verification completed, not the actual extent of their public documentation.

**What this means for Fulcrum.** The findings establish a substantial, geographically dispersed set of entry opportunities for document-based mapping. They support testing the product across multiple kinds of government work, rather than relying on a single country's publication practices or one service category.

**What they do not establish.** This is an evidence-supported lower bound, not a revenue-market estimate, a count of buyers, or proof of complete and accurate maps. Verification and language coverage are uneven. Some manuals are historical; publication does not establish current implementation. The remaining countries are not negative findings. Current-source reconciliation, actual mapping trials and government validation are needed before claims about workflow execution or a digital twin.
