Making government legible to machines, operable by agents, and accountable to people.

October 2026

Governments are bundles of structured processes. Who checks what, in which order, under which rule, by which deadline: this is the backbone of public administration. Most of it is public by law, in statutes, guidance, forms and reports. It is written for people to read, one document at a time.

AI agents can now carry out long sequences of work in software, within rules, using tools. For a government, that capability is usable only where the process has been made explicit enough for a machine to follow and for a person to check. Almost nowhere has it been.

We make government processes legible. We read the public record of a service and compress it into a map: steps, actors, rules, data, flows, each element tied to the passage it comes from. We then transform processes into workflows that can be operated by agents under human control, verified against the law it implements, and ultimately redesigned to improve service delivery and state capacity.

1 · Map

Build an accurate map of how a service runs today.

The goal is a precise picture of the process as it is, without interviews and without access to internal systems. Governments publish a great deal about how they work: laws, guidance, forms, decision templates, internal instructions, annual reports. Most of it is unstructured text. One document gives fragments. Many documents together give the whole process. The quantity of documents is what makes the map possible.

We read them with frontier models in two passes. First, atomic facts. Each fact carries a verbatim quote, and code checks that the quote exists in the source; a fact that cannot be found word for word is dropped. Second, the map, assembled only from verified facts. Steps that no document shows are marked inferred, not hidden.

The output is one JSON document per service, in a standard structure: steps, actors, rules with parameters, data sources, flows. Every element carries its provenance and a confidence score. The structure is the same for every service in every country, so maps can be compared and loaded by software.

2 · Encode

Build agents with harnesses to run the service.

The goal is to run the operations of a service with agents, under the authority's control. The map becomes the specification an agent harness loads: a tool for every register, every rule as a check, an approval gate wherever the law requires a person. Each step states what the agent may do: execute it (rules, register lookups, notifications), prepare it for a human (document checks, assessments), or leave it to people (formal decisions, the citizen's own actions).

This is more than a model of the process. The same harness that replays a past case can process a new one, and every run can be checked step by step against the law it implements. The authority keeps the decision, the accountability and the relationship with the citizen; the routine work runs on the encoded workflow. Small banks already buy their operations this way and keep the brand and the customer. Public services can move in the same direction, one process at a time.

3 · Redesign

Change the process to make it simpler, faster and more transparent.

The goal is state capacity: services that cost less to run, are easier to use, and produce better outcomes, with less of the public's time and the administration's. Today redesign means workshops: the process is reconstructed from interviews, drawn once, and argued over. With many services encoded in one structure, the shared work shows itself. Intake, identity, income checks, decision notices and annual reviews appear in every benefit, with rules that differ only in parameters and handovers that exist only because two offices once did.

Redesign becomes a comparison between maps. A proposed change is itself a map, with the same provenance and confidence, so it can be tested on synthetic cases before a law changes or a desk moves. The drawing shows the mechanical version: consecutive steps by the same actor doing the same kind of work merge, a check merges into the step that computes it, and loops are drawn once. We have not tested redesign with an agency yet.

What a step is

A map is a list of steps and the flows between them. Every step carries the same features, so maps can be compared, checked and loaded by software without interpretation.

Type
An event (something happens: an application arrives, a deadline passes), work by one actor, or a decision where the path splits.
Actor
Who performs it: citizen, caseworker, the authority's system, a third party, another authority.
External · internal
Whether the citizen sees the step, or it happens inside the authority.
Rules
The conditions and calculations the step applies, each a testable statement with parameters and legal basis. Rules are defined once per service and referenced by steps.
Inputs · outputs
What the step consumes and produces.
Data sources
The registers it reads, and how they are reached: by interface, by a person, or supplied by the citizen.
Channel · deadline
How the citizen interacts; the time limit that binds the step.
Agent mode
Execute, prepare for a human, or human only, with the reason.
Provenance
The passages the step rests on: source, section, verbatim quote. A step with none is marked inferred.
Confidence
Our expected probability that the step matches how the process really runs, from a fixed rubric. A responsible official can validate a step; validated steps count as certain.

General housing allowance (Kela, Finland)

By reading official documents, we mapped the processes of the general housing allowance: the benefit Finland pays to low-income households towards their housing costs, administered by Kela, the national social insurance institution.

For this demo we used public documents only. That gives high confidence for the steps citizens see and somewhat lower confidence for the steps inside Kela; access to internal documents would raise it.

2 · Agent-operable

What an agent could run today

    These are classifications, not measurements. For each step the model judged, from the rules and passages behind it, whether an agent could execute it, prepare it, or must leave it to people. Nobody has run an agent on this process yet. Two tests would turn the estimate into a measurement: a Kela process owner correcting the map, and an agent processing synthetic cases against Kela's own calculator.

    3 · Redesign

    Where redesign starts

    The map already shows the same kind of work repeated: four separate register retrievals, several decision notices, three different triggers that lead to the same recalculation. Which of these to merge, and what the law allows, is a conversation with Kela's process owners, not a drawing. The map is the agenda for it: here is your process as the public record describes it; correct it, then simplify it together.
